You can be perfectly indexed for a phrase nobody types. Everything after this section costs weeks. Crawler access, pages, links, dashboards — all of it pays only if somebody is already searching.

Two pages here, and they answer different questions. [The words people actually type](/demand/how-people-search/) is the harvest: where real phrasings come from, and how to read Yandex and Google. It also says what a paid panel is measuring when it hands you a volume number.

[Whether that audience pays for anything](/demand/will-they-pay/) is the second question. Paid competitors, ads on the query, what a pre-sale proves. Also how to read a large query that nobody bids on.

The paid tools live here rather than further along. Semrush and its peers describe a market you have not entered yet. They describe a site with no traffic far worse, and that is the case they are usually bought for.

Do both before the writing. A well-built page aimed at a phrase you invented is a month spent on your own vocabulary.
