## What we are solving

Three niches looked plausible at once, and I had no way to rank them. Each carried a different phrase, a different market and the same hope.

So I ran all of them through one instrument on one day. Semrush Keyword Overview, read on 2026-08-12, one database per market.

Everything below is that single pull. It is an estimate made from outside, and the limits are stated before the procedure.

## Steps

### Three niches, one market

All three phrases read in the United States database, on the same day.

| Phrase | Volume | CPC | Keyword Difficulty | Intent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `youtube transcript` | 60,500 | $0.86 | 70% | Navigational |
| `pdf to text` | 9,900 | $0.79 | 43% | Informational |
| `ai voice agent` | 1,900 | $16.18 | 40% | Informational |

Volume and money run in opposite directions here. `youtube transcript` has 32 times the searches of `ai voice agent`, and a click worth 19 times less.

The variation clusters do not rescue it. `youtube transcript` carries 4,900 keywords and 382,600 volume, `pdf to text` 15,900 keywords and 247,600.

Intent decides what you can charge for. Navigational means the person wants one specific free tool. Informational means they want to know how.

Neither of those two buys a subscription. The panel puts the commercial reading somewhere else entirely, and that is the next table but one.

The difficulty runs the wrong way as well. `youtube transcript` is scored 70% and `ai voice agent` 40%.

Semrush's note under the 70% asks for 126 high-authority referring domains. So the biggest niche of the three is the slowest to enter and the cheapest on arrival.

### The same phrase, four markets

`ai voice agent` in three English-speaking databases, and the Russian equivalent `голосовой бот` in the Russian one.

| Market | Phrase | Volume | CPC | Keyword Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| United States | `ai voice agent` | 1,900 | $16.18 | 40% |
| United Kingdom | `ai voice agent` | 260 | $9.75 | 48% |
| Germany | `ai voice agent` | 110 | $9.91 | 40% |
| Russia | `голосовой бот` | 390 | $2.65 | 64% |

Markets differ by more than the language on the page. The same idea pays $16.18 a click in the United States and $2.65 in Russian.

The Russian side is also the harder one. Its difficulty reads 64%, with a note asking for 196 referring domains to compete.

Nor is it a smaller room with more space in it. The Russian branch holds 376 variations at 2,700 total volume, against 2,969 and 32,000 in English.

The question block says the same. Russian: 51 questions, 110 volume. English: 181 questions, 1,200 volume.

That is about a twelfth of the addressable demand, at a sixth of the click price, and harder. The belief that a domestic-language niche is less contested does not survive this table.

### Two phrasings of one idea

Word order changes the reading. Both rows come from the United States database, on the same day.

| Phrase | Volume | CPC | Keyword Difficulty | Competitive density | Intent |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ai voice agent` | 1,900 | $16.18 | 40% | 0.52 | Informational |
| `voice ai agents` | 590 | $16.94 | 39% | 0.41 | Commercial |

The commercial reading sits on the smaller phrase, at the higher CPC. Which of the two you build the page around changes who arrives.

Ads settle it more firmly than any price. Fourteen advertisers are bidding on `ai voice agent` at once.

A CPC is one estimated price. An ad count is a queue of people repeating the purchase with their own money.

### The long specific phrases

Inside the English branch sit phrases with real volume and almost no difficulty.

| Phrase | Volume | Keyword Difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| `ai voice agent restaurants australia` | 1,600 | 10 |
| `ai voice agent cold calling b2b austria gdpr legal` | 590 | 9 |

The first one carries more volume than the head phrase in the United Kingdom. Neither of them is reachable from a keyword you invented at your desk.

### What these figures do not cover

- **Every number here is Semrush's estimate from outside**. It is not a measurement of anyone's property. The same panel missed a real position by more than twenty places: [what a paid rank tracker measures](/tools/paid-tools/).
- **Keyword Difficulty is scored against its own database**. So 40% and 64% are not strictly comparable across markets. Comparable within a market only.
- **Semrush does not cover Yandex**. For Russian-language demand that is a large hole, and this table is Google-only.
- **The English rows come from the United States database**. The country split next to the global volume is volume only, not CPC.

### The procedure

1. **Pick the phrasings before you open the tool** — your own word, a near-synonym, and the word a competitor uses.
   One phrasing is a sample of one. `ai voice agent` and `voice ai agents` came back with different intent.
2. **Read volume next to intent and CPC, never on its own** — three fields in one row.
   A headline of 60,500 at $0.86 and Navigational is a crowd looking for a free tool.
3. **Check whether anyone is bidding** — the Ads field, and the live results page.
   `ai voice agent` shows Ads 14. `youtube transcript` shows n/a, on 60,500 searches a month.
4. **Run the same phrase through each market database separately** — switch the country, do not translate in your head.
   Write the database name next to every figure. A number without its database is not comparable to anything.
5. **Open the variations list and sort for low difficulty** — that is where the long specific phrases are.
   Difficulty 9 and 10 on 590 and 1,600 volume is a different entry point from difficulty 40.
6. **Name the market out loud, with a number in the sentence** — one market, one reason.
   Mine reads: build for the United States, because the click is worth $16.18 and fourteen advertisers agree.

## What did not work

- **Picking the niche by search volume**. 60,500 looked like the goldmine of the three. That click is worth $0.86, the difficulty is 70%, and the intent is Navigational. I would have entered the slowest niche for the cheapest traffic.
- **Assuming the domestic-language market is the easier one**. The Russian phrase came back harder than the American one, 64% against 40%. It also pays $2.65 against $16.18. Both halves of that assumption were wrong in the same table.
- **Treating one phrasing as the whole niche**. I read `ai voice agent`, saw Informational, and nearly wrote the niche off. `voice ai agents` reads Commercial at $16.94, and it was one line further down the same screen.
- **Reading CPC without the ad count**. A price with nobody paying it is a forecast. Ads 14 is fourteen live bidders, and it is the field I checked last.
- **Comparing difficulty across databases as one scale**. I put 40% and 64% in a single row and drew a conclusion for a day. The score is relative to its own database, so that row compares nothing.
- **Reading the country split as a market list**. US 1,900, IN 880, UK 260, CA 170, AU 140, DE 110. The split is volume only, and India at 880 says nothing about what a click there costs.
- **Taking the panel for a measurement**. These are modelled figures about markets I have not entered. The atlas has one case where that estimate was off by more than twenty places, on a property I own.

## Verify

- Write one row per phrase with all four fields in it: volume, intent, CPC, ads. A row missing a field is not a decision yet.
- Re-run each phrase in its own market database and put the database name next to the number. Two numbers without databases are not a comparison.
- Count the ads by hand on the live results page, on two separate days. One day of ads proves nothing about a budget.
- Name the engine behind every row. For Russian-language demand check Wordstat as well: [the words people actually type](/demand/how-people-search/).
- State the market you picked in one sentence, with the figure that decided it. If the sentence works without the figure, you picked by taste.

In sum, the biggest number of the three was the worst niche of the three. Whether the audience behind the expensive click pays anything is the next question: [whether that audience pays for anything](/demand/will-they-pay/).
