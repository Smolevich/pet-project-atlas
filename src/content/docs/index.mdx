## I am not an SEO expert

I build side projects and I read their logs. This is what I saw in my own dashboards, panels and access logs, and how I went looking.

Your data will say something else. The procedure is the part that transfers. My numbers are just what one small project looked like on the day I measured it.

Sometimes the number is embarrassing. One click in three months. A file I told people to publish, fetched zero times by any AI agent. A mysterious crawler in my log that turned out to be my own `curl`.

Those are on the pages too. A route that only shows its wins teaches nobody where the walls are.

## What every page owes you

A page here has to hand you something you can run — a command, a query, a panel to open — instead of a principle to consider. Any number on it carries the day I measured it and the panel I read it off. Where I have neither, the number comes out of the page rather than getting rounded into a hedge, because a hedge looks like data and is not. And at the bottom sits a block called "What did not work", which is never empty: the build refuses a page where it is.

Two sections sit outside the route. [Tools](/tools/) says which skill to run for which symptom, and [Cases](/cases/) holds the bar a full write-up has to clear — one case is up so far, this site measured on itself.
