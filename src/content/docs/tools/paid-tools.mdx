## What we are solving

A paid panel hands you a table of positions. One row says position 20 for a phrase people actually type, and the product still gets nothing.

The question is which of those numbers you can act on. I ran Semrush over the site of a Telegram bot I run.

What these services are for, and which of them read Yandex, is one section earlier. See [what the paid panels actually measure](/demand/how-people-search/). This page is the measurement behind that overview.

## Steps

Read the whole panel in one pass before you read any single row.

| Panel field | What it said | What it is worth |
|---|---|---|
| Authority Score | 2, flagged "Lacks organic traffic" | A summary of the links, not a lever of its own |
| Organic Traffic | 0 | The outcome every position in the table is supposed to produce |
| Paid Traffic | 0 | Confirms nothing is being bought to cover the gap |
| Referring domains, Backlinks | 10 and 61 | Few sources, several links each. Sources are what counts |
| Organic Keywords | 11 | The pages are indexed and matched to real phrases |
| AI Visibility, Mentions | 0 and 0 | No answer in the tool's sample named the product |
| Cited Pages | 4, all under ChatGPT | Something was fetched and quoted somewhere. Nothing says where |
| Top Cited Sources | "We have no data to show" | The panel above it has no supporting detail |
| Key Topics | "No topics found. Domain is too small or relatively new." | The tool needs a footprint it can read. There is none |

The keyword table under it, worldwide, on the same day:

| Keyword | Position | Monthly volume |
|---|---|---|
| войс бот телеграм | 20 | 90 |
| бот который озвучивает текст | 47 | 170 |
| войс бот телеграм | 48 | 90 |
| бот говорит текст | 72 | 90 |
| бот для озвучки текста | 83 | 140 |

1. **Read Organic Traffic before any position** — it was 0.
   A table of positions above a traffic of 0 describes a site that nobody reaches.
2. **Pair the keyword count with that zero** — 11 keywords matched, the best row at position 20, traffic 0.
   That pair is a diagnosis. The pages exist and are indexed, and nothing off-site points at them.
3. **Notice a phrase that occupies two rows** — `войс бот телеграм` sits at position 20 and at position 48.
   One phrase, two answers. A row is not a verdict about the site, and neither number is an observation.
4. **Check the scope the panel is showing** — subdomain or root domain, worldwide or a single country tab.
   The database date sits next to it. Every figure moves when any of those change.
5. **Verify a position by hand before acting on it** — pin the country and the language, and run the query.

   ```text
   https://www.google.com/search?q=<query>&gl=ru&hl=ru
   ```

   Count the domains that come back, and look for your own among them.
6. **Do not count on a longer result page** — I asked for `num=30` and got exactly ten.
   Google no longer honours the parameter. Page two is now a click, not a query string.
7. **Name the engine the row is about** — Semrush reports Google, and these queries are Russian.
   Yandex is a second engine with no data in this panel. It cannot tell you what it is not reading.
8. **Read who owns page one before writing anything** — for the top query it was directories and a competitor.

   ```text
   t.me  botstat.io  voicee.ru  teamlogs.ru  telegram.me
   yagla.ru  help.cloud.just-ai.com  cybervoice.io  github.com  lumendatabase.org
   ```

   `botstat.io` and `yagla.ru` are listings, `cybervoice.io` is a competitor's product.
   A small product gets onto that page by being listed on it, not by outranking it.
9. **Read the AI Search block as two separate facts** — Mentions 0, and 4 cited pages, all from ChatGPT.
   Something was quoted, and no answer named the product. The panel does not say which pages or which prompts.
10. **Stop paying when the panel repeats a diagnosis you already have** — mine reports zeros and a page of directories.
    The work it points at is off-site. No subscription does that work for you.

## What did not work

- **Acting on the reported position 20**. I searched for `войс бот телеграм` with `gl=ru` and `hl=ru`, and ten domains came back without mine. Position 20 is the second page, so the report is not contradicted. It is unverified, and either way the user sees nothing.
- **Asking for a deeper result page to settle it**. `num=30` returned ten results. The parameter that made hand-checking cheap is gone, and every check past ten results now costs a click.
- **Reading one panel as "the search market"**. Semrush reports Google. These phrases are Russian, and Yandex serves that demand too. Not one row in the panel is about it, and the panel does not say so.
- **Treating Authority Score as a target**. Score 2 sits on 61 backlinks from 10 domains. The score is a summary of those links. Chasing the number means buying links, which is the one route that ends badly.
- **Reading Cited Pages 4 as visibility**. Mentions were 0 and Top Cited Sources was empty. Four pages were cited somewhere in ChatGPT's sample, and that is the whole claim.
- **Waiting for the tool to say what to write**. Key Topics answered "No topics found. Domain is too small or relatively new." A tool that reads an existing footprint cannot create the first one.
- **Buying the subscription before the off-site work**. It bought me a keyword list and a competitor's name. Both were reachable by hand, and neither changed what I had to do next.

## Verify

- Run the query yourself in a clean profile with the country and language pinned. Count the domains and look for your own.
- Ask for more than ten results and count what actually comes back.
- Check the panel's scope and database date against what you are about to quote from it.
- Run the same query on the second engine of your market by hand. No panel in this tool covers it.
- Compare the tool's Organic Traffic against clicks in Search Console next month. An estimate of 0 is the one figure that needs no trust.
