## What we are solving

I ran Semrush over the site of a Telegram bot I run, expecting a list of things to fix, and what came back was a table of positions. One row said position 20 for a phrase people really do type. The product was still getting nothing out of it.

So the question I actually had was which of those numbers I could act on. A day later I pulled the same query out of my own Search Console and it came back at 43.3. It also turned out not to be the same kind of number at all, and sorting that out took the second half of this page.

What these services are for, and which of them read Yandex, sits one section earlier: [what the paid panels actually measure](/demand/how-people-search/).

## Steps

I read the whole panel in one pass before letting myself look at any single row, because a row on its own always describes a better site than I actually have.

| Panel field | What it said | What it is worth |
|---|---|---|
| Authority Score | 2, flagged "Lacks organic traffic" | A summary of the links, not a lever of its own |
| Organic Traffic | 0 | The outcome every position in the table is supposed to produce |
| Paid Traffic | 0 | Confirms nothing is being bought to cover the gap |
| Referring domains, Backlinks | 10 and 61 | Few sources, several links each. Sources are what counts |
| Organic Keywords | 11 | The pages are indexed and matched to real phrases |
| AI Visibility, Mentions | 0 and 0 | No answer in the tool's sample named the product |
| Cited Pages | 4, all under ChatGPT | Something was fetched and quoted somewhere. Nothing says where |
| Top Cited Sources | "We have no data to show" | The panel above it has no supporting detail |
| Key Topics | "No topics found. Domain is too small or relatively new." | The tool needs a footprint it can read. There is none |

The keyword table under it, worldwide, on the same day:

| Keyword | Position | Monthly volume |
|---|---|---|
| войс бот телеграм | 20 | 90 |
| бот который озвучивает текст | 47 | 170 |
| войс бот телеграм | 48 | 90 |
| бот говорит текст | 72 | 90 |
| бот для озвучки текста | 83 | 140 |

### Which number I should have read first

Organic Traffic, and it was 0. A table of positions sitting on top of a traffic figure of zero describes a site nobody reaches, and I went straight to the rows anyway.

Then put the keyword count next to that zero: 11 keywords matched, the best row at position 20, traffic 0. The pair is the diagnosis. My pages exist, Google has them indexed and matched to phrases real people type, and nothing outside the site points at any of them.

### Why the same phrase sits in two rows

`войс бот телеграм` shows up twice in one export on one day, at position 20 and at position 48. One phrase, two answers, and I had quoted the better one.

Next to the table sits the scope — subdomain or root domain, worldwide or a single country tab — and beside that the date of the database behind it. Every figure in the panel moves when any one of those changes. I had been treating a row as a verdict about the site when it is closer to a reading taken under settings I never checked.

### What I saw when I ran the query myself

Pinning the country and the language, then running the thing by hand, costs a minute:

```text
https://www.google.com/search?q=<query>&gl=ru&hl=ru
```

Ten domains came back and mine was not among them. Position 20 is the second page, so nothing was contradicted — I simply could not confirm it either. I asked for `num=30` to see further down and got exactly ten results back. Google no longer honours the parameter, so page two is a click now rather than a string in the URL.

What was on page one turned out to matter more than my position on page two:

```text
t.me  botstat.io  voicee.ru  teamlogs.ru  telegram.me
yagla.ru  help.cloud.just-ai.com  cybervoice.io  github.com  lumendatabase.org
```

`botstat.io` and `yagla.ru` are listings, `cybervoice.io` is a competitor's product. A product my size gets onto that page by being listed on it, not by outranking the directories that are already there — which is a distribution job, not a writing one.

### Which engine the table is even about

Semrush reports Google, and these queries are Russian. Yandex serves a share of that demand and there is not one row about it anywhere in the panel, which the panel does not tell you either. A tool cannot report on an index it does not read, and I had been reading its silence as an absence of competitors.

### What the AI Search block is actually claiming

Two separate facts live in that block and for a while I read them as one. Mentions 0, and Cited Pages 4, all four under ChatGPT.

So something of mine was fetched and quoted somewhere inside the tool's own sample, and no answer in that sample named the product. Which pages, and on which prompts, it does not say.

### What my own property says about the same query

Semrush estimates a position from the outside. Search Console counts the impressions Google actually served, so I went and pulled the property itself through the Search Console API — dimension `query`, 2026-05-12 to 2026-08-12, three months.

```bash
curl -sS -X POST \
  'https://searchconsole.googleapis.com/webmasters/v3/sites/sc-domain:example.com/searchAnalytics/query' \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"startDate":"2026-05-12","endDate":"2026-08-12","dimensions":["query"],"rowLimit":100}'
```

| Query | Clicks | Impressions | CTR | Position |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| озвучка детским голосом бот телеграм | 1 | 1 | 100% | 49.0 |
| telegram text to speech | 0 | 24 | 0% | 20.5 |
| bot voice | 0 | 12 | 0% | 41.0 |
| smolevich | 0 | 8 | 0% | 15.4 |
| telegram voice | 0 | 6 | 0% | 51.5 |
| stt модели | 0 | 1 | 0% | 1.0 |
| best stt models 2026 | 0 | 1 | 0% | 10.0 |
| telegram premium voice message transcription | 0 | 1 | 0% | 9.0 |
| moonshine vs parakeet | 0 | 1 | 0% | 11.0 |
| войс бот телеграм | 0 | 6 | 0% | 43.3 |

That is the head of the table, and the whole period comes to 74 queries, 278 impressions, 1 click. Every figure is what the API returned. An earlier version of this table had a dash where the 6 now sits, and I quoted the 43.3 without it.

### What an average of six impressions is worth

Here are both readings of the one query, side by side:

| Query | The panel said | My Search Console said | Measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| `войс бот телеграм` | position 20, monthly volume 90 | position 43.3, on 6 impressions and 0 clicks | 2026-08-11 panel, 2026-08-12 property |

They are different quantities. Semrush estimates a position on one day out of its own database, and Search Console averages the positions of the impressions it really served. Filtering the same pull to that one query and asking for the days and the pages behind it is where the row falls apart:

```bash
curl -sS -X POST \
  'https://searchconsole.googleapis.com/webmasters/v3/sites/sc-domain:example.com/searchAnalytics/query' \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $TOKEN" \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"startDate":"2026-05-12","endDate":"2026-08-12",
       "dimensions":["date","page"],
       "dimensionFilterGroups":[{"filters":[
         {"dimension":"query","operator":"equals","expression":"войс бот телеграм"}]}]}'
```

Six impressions, on three days out of eighty-nine: two on 23 June at position 53, three on 28 July at 43.3, one on 31 July at position 24. Two different pages answered the query as well — `/ru` took four impressions at position 38, one article took two at position 54.

So 43.3 is the mean of six impressions spread over positions 24 to 53 and two pages, which is a number the API will print for any query it showed at all. The panel's 20 lands four places from the best of those days and thirty-three from the worst, and I cannot say from this row which instrument was closer to the truth.

Pull impressions with position, always. What survives here is smaller and firmer than the comparison I first published: the query produced six impressions and no clicks in three months, and no reading of the panel changes that.

### Which position brought a click and which did not

`telegram text to speech` sat at position 20.5 with 24 impressions and 0 clicks. Two dozen people were shown my result on page two and not one of them clicked, which is what page two is worth on a query like this.

The one click of the whole quarter came from position 49, on a single impression, on `озвучка детским голосом бот телеграм`. That is the bottom of page five. The phrase was long and specific and my page matched it exactly, and specificity beat position by a distance no title rewrite would have covered.

### Is position 1.0 a fact about demand or about me

`stt модели` came back at position 1.0 with 1 impression and 0 clicks, on 9 August, and I first read that as a phrase nobody types. Impressions count how often Google showed my result, not how often somebody typed the phrase — so the row is a fact about my page and says nothing about demand.

I went and checked the phrase itself instead. Yandex Wordstat gives 56 requests, all regions, 10 July to 8 August 2026. The conclusion held, but the evidence for it is the 56 and not the 1: [the words people type](/demand/how-people-search/) and [whether they pay](/demand/will-they-pay/).

### Whether the subscription is still worth paying for

Mine reports zeros and a page of directories, and both of those I already knew. The work it points at is off-site, and no subscription does that work for me.

So I publish the totals with the zeros in them: 74 queries, 278 impressions, 1 click over three months. That is the honest shape of a small project in search, and I would rather have read someone else's before I read my own.

## What did not work

- **Acting on the reported position 20**. I searched for `войс бот телеграм` with `gl=ru` and `hl=ru`, and ten domains came back without mine. Position 20 is the second page, so the report is not contradicted — it is unverified, and either way the user sees nothing.
- **Asking for a deeper result page to settle it**. `num=30` returned ten results. The parameter that made hand-checking cheap is gone, and every check past ten results costs me a click now.
- **Reading one panel as "the search market"**. Semrush reports Google, my phrases are Russian, and Yandex serves that demand too. Not one row in the panel is about it, and the panel does not say so anywhere.
- **Treating Authority Score as a target**. Score 2 sits on 61 backlinks from 10 domains, and the score is a summary of those links. Chasing the number means buying links, which is the one route that ends badly.
- **Reading Cited Pages 4 as visibility**. Mentions were 0 and Top Cited Sources was empty. Four pages were cited somewhere in ChatGPT's sample, and that is the whole of what the panel claims.
- **Waiting for the tool to say what to write**. Key Topics answered "No topics found. Domain is too small or relatively new." A tool that reads an existing footprint cannot create the first one for me.
- **Buying the subscription before the off-site work**. It bought me a keyword list and a competitor's name, both of which I could have got by hand in an evening, and neither changed what I had to do next.
- **Leaving the reported position unchecked against my own property**. The hand-check could not disprove position 20, so I let it stand. One API call brought back what Google had actually served on my own property, and that was the pull worth making first.
- **Publishing 43.3 with a dash where the impressions go**. I quoted an average and left out the one field that says whether it means anything. It is six impressions on three days, positions 24 to 53, served by two different pages. With the 6 in the row, nobody would have read the number the way I wrote it.
- **Calling a one-day panel estimate and a ninety-day property average a comparison of positions**. They are aggregated differently, and a gap between them is what you get when both instruments are working fine. I published that gap as a finding about the panel.
- **Reading one impression as a statement about demand**. `stt модели` came back at position 1.0 and I wrote that nobody types it. Search Console counts how often my result was shown, which is a fact about me. Wordstat counts the query itself, and it says 56 a month — same conclusion, on the instrument that can carry it.
- **Expecting a decent position to produce clicks on its own**. Position 20.5, 24 impressions, 0 clicks. I spent an evening hunting for a title and snippet fix that does not exist on page two.

## Verify

- Run the query yourself in a clean profile with the country and the language pinned, count the domains and look for your own.
- Ask for more than ten results and count what actually comes back.
- Before you quote anything out of the panel, check the scope it is showing and the date of the database behind it.
- Run the same query by hand on the second engine of your market. No panel in this tool covers it.
- Next month, put the tool's Organic Traffic estimate next to clicks in Search Console. An estimate of 0 is the one figure that needs no trust.
- Pull the same query from your own property over a period long enough to hold impressions, then put both positions in one row.
- Read impressions and clicks next to every position you quote. A position with no clicks under it is not a result yet.
